A classic game, played the southern way

Tiến Lên means "go forward", and that is the whole idea of the game: every play has to beat the one before it, and the first player to get rid of all thirteen cards wins. The rules fit on one page, yet a single hand can turn around in seconds when someone drops a pair of 2s or answers with three pairs in a row. That mix of simple rules and sudden swings is why the game has stayed popular for generations, and why it is often the first card game Vietnamese children learn from their parents and grandparents.

Tiến Lên Nova plays the southern rules (Tiến Lên Miền Nam), the version most people in southern Vietnam and in Vietnamese communities around the world grew up with. That includes chopping 2s (chặt heo), consecutive pairs (đôi thông), four of a kind (tứ quý), instant wins (tới trắng), and the end-of-hand rules many families play with, such as cóng and thối. If your family plays a little differently, you can switch several of these house rules on or off before you play.

How a hand works, in one minute

Four players each get thirteen cards. The lowest card is the 3 and the highest is the 2; among cards of the same rank, hearts beat diamonds, diamonds beat clubs and clubs beat spades. The player who leads can play a single card, a pair, a triple, a run of three or more cards in order, or a few other combinations. Everyone after them must play the same kind of combination, only higher, or pass. When everyone else has passed, the last player to play starts a new round with anything they like. Get rid of your cards first and you win the hand. The full guide covers every detail.

Free, and play money only

Tiến Lên Nova is a game of skill and fun, not gambling. Points in the game are shown as chips, but chips are play money with no cash value. They cannot be bought, sold, or exchanged for money or prizes, and there is no way to win anything of real value. Everyone gets free chips every day. The game is meant for adults aged 18 and over, and is free to play.
