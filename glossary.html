Tiến Lên glossary

Players use many Vietnamese words at the Tiến Lên table. Here is what they mean, in alphabetical order of the Vietnamese term.

Ba đôi thông (three consecutive pairs)
Three pairs in rank order, such as 6-6, 7-7, 8-8. Played like any combination, and it can also chop a single 2.
Bài rác / lá lẻ (single, "trash card")
A single card, especially a low one that does not belong to any combination. Getting rid of these early is a key part of strategy.
Bét (last place)
The player who finishes fourth. They lose the most points for the hand.
Bỏ lượt / bỏ (pass)
To decline to play. After passing you sit out the rest of the round, except that you may still chop when the rules allow.
Bốn đôi thông (four consecutive pairs)
Four pairs in rank order, such as 9-9, 10-10, J-J, Q-Q. The strongest bomb: it chops a single 2, a pair of 2s, three pairs or four of a kind, and it may be played out of turn.
Chặt (to chop)
Beating a 2, a pair of 2s or another bomb with a bomb, outside the normal order of play. The chopped player pays points to the chopper.
Chặt chồng (chop on a chop)
Chopping a bomb that has just chopped something. With the refund house rule, the first chopper gives back what they won.
Cóng (shut out)
Being left without having played a single card when another player goes out. The cóng player finishes last and pays an extra penalty.
Đánh (to play)
To put a card or combination on the table.
Đôi (pair)
Two cards of the same rank.
Đôi heo (pair of 2s)
Two 2s together, the strongest pair. It can only be beaten by a higher pair of 2s or chopped by four consecutive pairs.
Đôi thông (consecutive pairs)
Three or more pairs in rank order.
Heo (pig, the 2)
The nickname for the 2, the highest card. A red heo (diamonds or hearts) is worth more than a black one (spades or clubs) when chopped.
Heo cuối (the last 2)
A house rule: if a player goes out with a 2 as their last play, it can still be chopped to take the turn, but no points are paid, because that player has already won.
Lượt / vòng (turn, round)
A round lasts from the first play until everyone else passes. The last player to play then starts the next round.
Miền Nam / Miền Bắc (south / north)
The two main families of rules. Tiến Lên Miền Nam, played here, is the southern version and the most common worldwide. The northern version has stricter rules about matching suits.
Nhất, nhì, ba (first, second, third)
The finishing places. They decide how many points each player wins or loses.
Sám / bộ ba (triple)
Three cards of the same rank.
Sảnh (straight)
Three or more cards in rank order, in any suits. A 2 cannot be part of a straight.
Sảnh rồng (dragon straight)
A straight with every rank from 3 to ace in one hand. It wins instantly.
Sáu đôi (six pairs)
A dealt hand containing six pairs. It wins instantly.
Thối (stuck cards penalty)
The last-place player pays extra for 2s and bombs still in their hand when the hand ends.
Tiến lên ("go forward")
The name of the game: every play must go higher than the one before.
Tới / về (to go out)
To play your last card. "Về nhất" means to go out first.
Tới trắng (instant win)
Winning as soon as the cards are dealt, with a hand such as four 2s, six pairs or a dragon straight.
Tứ quý (four of a kind)
All four cards of one rank. A bomb that chops a single 2, three pairs or a lower four of a kind.
Tứ quý heo (four 2s)
All four 2s in one hand: an instant win.

Tiến Lên Nova is a free card game for entertainment. Chips are play money with no cash value: they cannot be bought, sold or exchanged for money or prizes, and there is no way to win real money. For players aged 18 and over.
